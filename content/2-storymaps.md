## Introduction

In this activity, you'll learn how to you ArcGIS StoryMaps to effectively communicate your research. By the end, you'll be able to:
- Navigate to the StoryMaps page within ArcGIS Online
- Create a StoryMap and customize its appearance
- Add text, media, maps, and other types of content to your StoryMap
- Organize your content into a well-structured, compelling narrative

---

## Getting Started

Before you begin, Download the [images in this folder.](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1d37-5oRtXhh3R7oMPzSF357CiaF5QQ7L?usp=sharing){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} We'll be using these images to create our StoryMaps.

Navigate to the [ISU ArcGIS Online Homepage](https://isugisf.maps.arcgis.com/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and log in if you aren't already. In the nav bar at the top, click on the grid of nine dots. Find the teal **StoryMaps** icon -- it should be one of the first few icons at the top.

You'll now find yourself on the StoryMaps homepage. If you've used StoryMaps before, you'll find any StoryMaps you've made on this page. If you haven't, the page will be empty. 

To create a StoryMap, click **+ Create** in the top right corner. Choose **Story**. 

You now have a blank StoryMap. Your first step should be to give it a descriptive title and subtitle, and add a cover image. To add a cover image, click **Add cover image or video**. Choose **Browse your files** and navigate to the location where you saved your images. Choose an image you wish to use, and click **Add**. Next, click the **gear icon** over the cover image, select **Accessibility**, and add descriptive alt text. Remember to add alt text to **all** images and videos in your StoryMap. 

Now that we have some content, we can customize its appearance. In the nav bar at the top, click **Design**. You can choose from one of six **Cover** styles -- try out each and choose which one you like best. For each style, there are further customizations you can make by clicking either the **easel and paintbrush** icon, or the **arrows** icon, located near your title. 

Next, under **Optional story sections**, you should turn **Navigation** on. This will automatically create a nav bar under your title card that will allow your viewers to navigate to different sections of your StoryMap. We'll cover how to create headings that will appear in this nav bar shortly. 

Finally, you can choose from one of six pre-set themes, affecting the color pallette, text, and other visual features of the StoryMap. You can also browse user-created themes or create your own. For now, choose one of the pre-set themes. You can always change this later.

## Adding text

in StoryMaps, text, along with all other content types, is added in blocks. To add your first text block, click the teal **+** button and under **Basic**, select **Text**. 

This first text block is going to be a heading. Since it's our first section, let's call it "Introduction". Highlight that word, and you should see some text editor options pop up. Click on **Paragraph** and change it to **Heading 1**. Paragraph is the style of text you'll use for regular pieces of your narrative -- Headings denote new sections. You may have noticed that when you changed this text to Heading 1, it appeared in your nav bar. **All Heading 1s in your StoryMap will automatically appear in your nav bar, giving readers an easy way to get to specific parts of the story.**

Add another text block. This one will be a regular paragraph style block, where you can start your narrative. No need to write a whole essay right away -- just a few words will work for now, you can always come back to edit any of your blocks later.

Don't worry to much about putting a block in exactly the right place relative to your other content. You can always to back and insert new blocks between two blocks you've already created!

## Adding images and other media

Adding images to your map works similarly to adding text. Click the **+** button, then under **Media** select **Image**. 

The window that opens should look familiar to you -- it's very similar to the window we saw when we were adding our cover image. There are two small differences though: 
1. For every image _other than_ your cover image, you can select **Link** and paste a link to an image from an image hosting service like Flickr, instead of uploading from your own device. 
2. Once you've added at least one image to your story, you'll be able to select that image again from the **This story** tab. This is a quick and easy way to reuse content. 

For now, we're going to stick with the **Upload** tab. Just like when you chose your cover image, click **Browse your files** and choose an image to add. You can use the options bar that appears when you hover over the image to edit it or change its position, and you can add a caption by clicking on the caption prompt below the image. 

Click the **+** button once more and scroll to view all **Media** options. Sometimes you want to be able to display more than one image side by side. In this case, you'll want to use the **Image gallery** feature. Using this option, you can choose several images at once -- I'm going to select all of our example images -- to display in a gallery view. 

To add video, click the **+** button and find **Video** under **Media**. While the StoryMaps platform allows you to upload a video file just like images, this is **not recommended**. StoryMaps does not handle large file sizes well. If you have a video you wish to include in a StoryMap, it is highly recommended that you upload it to a video hosting site like YouTube and link to it. 

There are many other kinds of media you can add. These include panoramic, 360 degree images, audio, HTML embeds, and more. Selecting **Swipe** allows you to compare two images or maps side-by-side -- useful for before and after images or showing differences or similarities between two maps. **Timeline** allows you to build a simple timeline of events as a visual accompaniment to your narrative.

## Adding maps

To add a map to your StoryMap, click the **+** button, then find **Map** under **Data Visualization**. 

You have two primary options for maps to add: 
- Maps you created yourself in ArcGIS Online Map Viewer
- Express maps

By default, you should see any maps you've created yourself in this window. You can click on one to choose it. On the next screen, you can adjust the default center point and zoom level by panning and scrolling. When you're happy with how the map looks, hit **Save**. Your map will appear in your StoryMap, and your audience will be able to take advantage of all its interactive features.

Sometimes you don't have time to create a map in Map Viewer, or the content you want to display on a map is simple enough that using that platform seems like overkill. In these cases, you can use **Express Maps**. 

Click the **+** button, select **Map**, and then, at the top of the page, choose **Create express map**. 

Here, you can create a simple map using points, lines, and polygons. Add a point by clicking on the **pin** icon in the bar along the top. You can assign an image to that point, give it a title and description, and change its style. When you're, done, hit Save. You can also create numbered points by clicking the **circled 1** icon. Each subsequent time you click the map using numbered points, the number will increase. 

You can use the other icons in the bar along the top to create different kinds of lines and polygons. Each of these can also be assigned images, titles, and descriptions. When you're happy with your Express Map, click **Save** in the bottom right.

## Adding immersive features

StoryMaps features two kinds of content it calls "Immersive". These blocks take up the whole screen and combine the other types of media we've used -- primarily text, images, and maps -- into one cohesive whole. 

**Sidecars** are useful when you want to dive deep into one specific topic. To add one, click the **+** button, and find **Sidecar** under **Immersive**. 

You have three aesthetic options. For now, choose **Docked**.

Sidecars are built by creating slides. To create your first slide, add some text in the bar along the left, then add an image or map on the right. To create your second slide, click the **+** button in the bottom right. 

This tool can be useful for showing different views of the same map -- for each slide you can set different desired center points and zoom levels for your map, highlighting different views. It can also be useful if you want to focus on one place -- maybe your first slide has a map to orient your audience, and all subsequent slides include images from that place. 

**Map tours** are fundamentally rooted in a single map. To create one, click the **+** button below your Sidecar, and choose **Map tour**. You have three options -- for now, select **Start from scratch**. You'll be prompted to choose from one of four layouts. The first two are best when you want your audience to move through the tour in a set order. The last two are best when you have many locations to highlight and order is not important. We'll choose Map Focused for now. 

At first, this interface looks similar to the Sidecar interface. The primary difference is that there's a blank map on your screen by default, instead of the option to choose a map or image. Add an image and title to your first slide, then, under the title card, click **Add location**. 

You can use the search bar at the top right to choose the location you want tied to your first slide. When you're happy with the selected location, click **Add to map**, then **save**.

Add a new slide just like you did in Sidecar. Each time you add a new location, you'll notice that the default zoom level of the map changes. Map tours automatically determine zoom level based on the locations of each point you add to the map.

## Previewing your StoryMap

Now that you've added a variety of content to your StoryMap, it's a good idea to preview it to see how it will appear to your audience. 

In the bar along the top of the page, click **Preview**. Select **Yes, continue** to be taken to the preview page. Here, you can scroll through your story to see exactly how it will look and function to your audience. 

At the bottom right, you can change the view to see how your StoryMap will appear on different sizes of screens. It's important to test out each of these -- something may look perfect on a desktop, but be skewed or out of place on a smartphone. 

When you're done previewing, hit your browser's back arrow.

## Publishing and sharing your StoryMap

When your StoryMap is finished, you'll need to publish it in order for others to be able to view it. 

In the bar along the top of the page, click **Publish**. Here, you can set your Sharing level to either **Private** (only visible to you), **Organization** (visible to anyone at ISU), or **Everyone (Public)**. If you're collaborating on this story with others, you can add it to a Group to allow your partners to edit it as well. You can also choose options that will allow others to duplicate your story and use it as a starting point for their own work, and for your StoryMap to show up in results pages on search engines. 

When you're happy with these settings, click **Publish**. On the next screen, you can copy the link to your published StoryMap, share it on social media, view it, or go back and edit it further. 

If you've published a StoryMap but no longer wish for others to be able to see it, you can always return to the **Publish** page and change your sharing level to Private.

Using the principles of good storytelling that we discussed earlier, either **edit this StoryMap** or **create a new one** and create a logical structure for a good narrative flow.
